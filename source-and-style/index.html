Achilles and Penthesilea

This page presents an ancient Greek vase from The Metropolitan Museum of Art that depicts the mythological encounter between the Greek hero Achilles and Penthesilea, the queen of the Amazons.

About the Object

The object is a terracotta hydria, or water jar, attributed to the Berlin Painter. The scene depicts Achilles and Penthesilea. Penthesilea was the queen of the Amazons and fought on the Trojan side during the Trojan War. According to the myth, Achilles killed Penthesilea and fell in love with her at the moment he delivered the fatal blow.

The image demonstrates how Greek mythology was represented through visual culture. Rather than existing only as written stories, myths were also represented on everyday and ceremonial objects such as painted pottery.

Why This Object Matters

  • It represents a scene from Greek mythology.
  • It connects mythology with ancient Greek visual culture.
  • It shows how mythological characters could be represented through vase painting.
  • It provides a physical object through which a myth was preserved and transmitted.
